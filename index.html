What it does

Eight tools that share one set of numbers

Change a category on a transaction and the budget, the cash-flow calendar and the retirement projection all move with it. That is the whole point — the parts of a financial life are not separate, so the tools shouldn't be either.

Financial dashboard

Income, spending, savings, debts and retirement goals in one view — with the single number that matters most up front: how long the money lasts.

Import and categorise

Upload a CSV from your bank, paste rows in, or add spending by hand. Every guessed category shows the word it matched, so you can correct it with confidence.

Personalised budgeting

Adjustable budgets for essentials, discretionary spending, debt payments and savings — or set them all at once from what you actually spent over the last three months.

Cash-flow calendar

Income and bills mapped day by day with a running balance, so a shortfall shows up while there is still time to move a payment rather than after a fee has landed.

Savings-goal planner

Emergencies, retirement, travel, helping family — each with a target, a monthly contribution and the month it actually finishes at the rate you are going.

Retirement income planner

Expected spending set against pension, Social Security, part-time work and savings withdrawals — year by year, with every assumption written out in plain language.

Scenario simulator

Retire two years later. Work part-time to seventy. Claim Social Security at seventy. Watch markets disappoint. Drag one slider and see the whole projection respond.

Alerts & scam checker

Unusual charges flagged with the reason spelled out, plus a checker for the message or phone call you are not sure about. Mark anything as recognised, or as needing a look.

How it works

Three steps, then it keeps up with you

Bring the money in

Import a CSV, paste rows, or type them. Categories are guessed from the description and shown with the word that matched, so correcting one takes a second.

Say what you mean to do

Set budgets, list the bills that repeat, name the things you are saving for, and write down what you expect retirement to cost.

Ask the hard questions

Does this month hold together? Does the plan reach ninety-two? What happens if it doesn't? And is that charge from Tuesday really yours?

Built to be read, not just looked at

A few decisions that shape every screen.

  • Every chart has a table. Values are never locked inside a hover tooltip, and every chart can be read with the keyboard.
  • Every projection shows its assumptions. Return rates, inflation, claiming adjustments and what is not modelled — including tax.
  • Every alert explains itself. A flag you can't interrogate is a flag that teaches people to ignore flags.
  • Large type, high contrast, light and dark. Built for the people most often on the receiving end of financial fraud.

Financial safety

Two different problems, both worth naming

A charge you didn't make is a bank's problem. A payment you were talked into making is not — and it is by far the more expensive of the two. Foresight treats them separately.

Unusual transaction alerts

Pattern checks against your own history, not a generic rule book.

  • Much larger than usual. A charge several times your typical spend in that category.
  • Possible duplicate. The same merchant and the same amount within a few days.
  • Hard-to-reverse payment. Gift cards, wires, crypto, instant transfers — the methods that cannot be clawed back.
  • First time, out of area, card not present. Individually ordinary; together, worth a second look.

Mark anything as recognised and it stops appearing. Mark it as needing investigation and you get the steps — and the deadlines — for disputing it.

Scam-awareness checker

Paste the message. It names the tactic, and shows the exact words that matched.

  • “Move your money to a safe account.” There is no such account. This instruction is fraud, every time.
  • “Don't tell anyone.” Asking you to keep it from your family or the teller is one of the strongest single indicators there is.
  • “Read me the code we just sent.” A one-time code is the key to the account. Nobody legitimate asks for it.
  • Urgency, authority, secrecy. The three ingredients almost every scam needs, whatever story it is wrapped in.

Fourteen patterns in total. The checker never returns a verdict — it tells you what matched and what to do next, and leaves the judgement with you.

About

An open demonstration, not a financial service

Foresight is a self-contained front-end project. There is no server, no account system and no connection to any bank. The demo ships with six months of generated sample data; anything you change is written to your own browser's local storage and nowhere else. Clearing your browser data clears Foresight with it.

Everything — the categorisation rules, the projection model, the anomaly checks and the scam-pattern library — runs as plain JavaScript modules in the page. No build step, no framework, no third-party scripts, no analytics.

The code is on GitHub, and the parts worth reusing are the parts that are usually hidden: the keyword rules that categorise a transaction, the year-by-year retirement model, and the fourteen scam patterns with the reasoning behind each one.

Please read this part. Foresight is an educational demonstration. It is not financial, tax, investment or legal advice, and it is not a substitute for a qualified professional who knows your situation.

The projections are deliberately simple. They assume one steady rate of return every year, which no real market provides. They do not model tax, sequence-of-returns risk, long-term care costs, or anything else that tends to decide real outcomes. Treat the shape of the answer as useful and the decimal places as fiction.

The safety tools find patterns, not truth. A sophisticated scam may match none of them. An ordinary message from your bank may match several. Use them as a prompt to stop and check through a channel you choose yourself.

If money is at stake right now, call your bank on the number printed on your card and ask for the fraud team. In the US you can also report to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov, or call the National Elder Fraud Hotline on 833-372-8311.
